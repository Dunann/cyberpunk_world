### Strongest reason it will fail
I-016 was adopted on G2, G3 and B3 because of the lit parks seen from the air: uplit trees, LED walk curbs, glowing plaza floors and plaza rings (I-016 result review: "aerial gain only"). Parts 3–4 remove most of that light. Uplighting drops from about 93% to about 25% of park trees, the `plazaM`/`plazaEm` LED floors go, a quarter of the plazas and their lantern rings are dropped, and the Silk star lattice is cut to 25%. What replaces it (lobed crowns, clusters instead of rows, stone terraces) can hardly be seen at night from G3's or B3's height. In `G3-aerial-city.jpg` the Lantern Park reads as a line of glowing plaza rings and lit dots; trees there are a few pixels wide. Removing the light while adding small mesh detail is the opposite of what these judges rewarded. In addition, the declared target rule (8% of tiles changed) will probably also select G1 and A5, because the forest floor, slope thinning and species changes alter many tiles there. On those poses the spec itself forecasts "tie or slight win", and ties count against the 2/3 rule. A plausible outcome is G2 win, G4 win, B3 split, G3 slight loss, G1 and A5 ties: 2 of 6, not adopted.

### Other concerns
- **Step 6 is already in the code.** `terrainShade.js` lines 285–297 already replace the crown domes with a dark matte forest floor near the camera: `domeK = smoothstep(220.0, 650.0, dist)`, with the comment "Close up (where real trees stand on it) the ground is a dark, matte forest floor". The spec describes this as new. The only real change left is moving the 220 m start outward. That matters at G4, whose left bank is 200–600 m away. The near ring thins trees there (`keep = clamp((210/d)^2, 0.2, 1)`, survivors grown by at most 1.75). Canopy coverage is about 84% at 400 m and about 61% at 600 m, and the dome shading is what fills the gaps. With a flat dark floor, the "bump-texture hillside" becomes sparse trees on a black slope, which critics already flag as "black voids". The fix needs denser trees at 200–650 m, which costs GPU time and is missing from the cost table.
- **The far-LOD GPU estimate is not credible.** LOD 2/3 goes from 20 triangles (`blob` subdivision 0) to about 250 (3 × 80 plus trunk), 12 times more. At the spec's own figure of about 15k far instances, plus near-ring LOD 2 and fixed trees beyond 420 m, that is 2–4M triangles per frame. The tree pipelines use `cull: 'none'` (`vegetation.js` 551–552), so back faces are rasterized too. A 2-GPC AD107 laptop chip sets up about 2 triangles per clock (about 4 Gtri/s). That makes 0.3–0.8 ms, not the 0.03–0.06 ms claimed, and it can exceed the idea's +0.20 ms cap. The "−0.3 to −0.5 ms when hiding trees" figure from I-016 was measured on a shared GPU (±0.5 ms noise, I-016 spec line 59), with 20-triangle far meshes. It says nothing about a mesh with 12 times the triangles.
- **The rng(6262) claim fails as written.** In `planPark.js`, `secondaryPaths` picks spots from the lit-distance and plaza fields (`onWalk` reads `R.plaza`) and draws `R6()` in `curvePath` for each path it adds. Ponds also test `R.plaza` before drawing `R6()`. Dropping plazas, changing their radius to 9–16 m, or widening walks shifts every later R6 draw, so the walks, ponds and terrace spurs change. Plazas that land far from their junction (`flattest`) also add or remove `link` walks. This only stays identical if the new plaza and walk-widening shapes are a post-layout overlay with separate fields, and the terrain shader needs those fields too. The spec says neither.
- **The plaza variety rule mostly produces one type.** Lantern Park's `river` plazas sit on the flattest ground within 30 m of each cross walk's junction with the river walk (`plazaAt(w.pts[0], w.pts[1], 14, 'river')`). So almost all of them meet "within 30 m of the river walk" and become timber decks. The hash-based choices (none, terrace, court) then apply only to the `upper` plazas, which exist on every other cross walk. The judged defect "regularly spaced round plazas" becomes "regularly spaced decks", now without the LED ring or the lit 8-sided pavilion (`light: kind === 'river'`). The deck "to the embankment edge" also crosses the river walk, its lamps and its willow row.
- **Check 21 contradicts the plan.** It requires A1–A6, B1, B2 and B4 to match main when trees are hidden. But step 6 changes terrain shading within 650 m wherever forest > 0.05 (A5 has forest to the right of the station), and the spec lists A5 as a possible target. Either A5 leaves the check, or step 6 is skipped.
- **The floating audit checks the wrong surface.** Step 22 compares trunk bottoms with `tHeight` samples. But the drawn terrain is a CDLOD mesh with 2 m vertex spacing out to 368 m, 4 m to 736 m, 8 m to 1472 m and 16 m beyond (`terrain.js`, LEAF 64, N 32, K 5.75). On convex slopes at 650–2100 m the mesh lies metres below `tHeight`, so the audit will report 0 failures while far trees can still float. Step 2 also adds little. LOD 0/1 trunks already start at local y −0.5 × scale under `h − 0.3`, so their downhill side is buried even at slope 1.0 (0.34 × scale radius against 0.3 + 0.5 × scale sinking). The real grounding fix is step 3 (trunk and low lobe on LOD 2/3), and it should be sized for the mesh error at each distance.
- **Street trees change in A4/B2-type views.** The 948 street trees are species 0 at scale 0.62–0.74, on sidewalks at `hw + 1.9` m from the road centre. Step 7 makes 20% of them wide banyans, which can overhang roads and touch facades. The spec says these poses will not change, but burst-u12 and street held-outs could. Banyans should be kept off street trees (dome or upright oval only).
- **Per-list caps sized from the fly-through can drop trees at poses.** `emit` silently drops instances over `CAP`. G1 (an overlook into forest) and the rule-placed forest cameras can exceed the fly-through peak, which would leave holes in the forest. Size the caps from the maximum over the fly-through, all 14 poses and the held-out cameras, plus a margin.
- **Shading constants are per species, and the new forms need them too.** AO height uses `select(13.0, 14.0, sp == 1.0)`, and the culling radii are `11/14 × s` and `9 × s`. Banyan (short trunk, wide) and upright oval (tall) need their own values, or the self-shadowing gradient and culling will be wrong.
- **The "lowest lobe within 4 m of the ground" rule is unclear.** It does not say whether 4 m is local or world. Far-ring scale reaches about 2.5, so 4 m local is 10 m in the world, and the gap stays.

### What would make it stronger
- Split the idea. Run parts 1–2 (meshes, grounding, slope rule, shrubs) as their own idea, with a declared target list and the quick test as written. Treat parts 3–4 as a separate park follow-up that keeps the light budget: keep uplighting at least at I-016's level of visible light (fewer but brighter uplights, lit planters, lit deck edges), and test it on aerial held-out cameras such as H1, H3 and H7 at G3-like heights before any eval capture.
- Far LOD: 3 lobes at subdivision 0 (about 60 triangles) with smoothed normals plus a 6–8-triangle trunk stub, and `cull: 'back'` for the opaque LOD 2/3 pipeline (`finish()` already orients the closed blobs). Measure the gbuffer pass at G1, G4 and the forest held-outs with `--stats` before choosing subdivision 1.
- Keep `domeK` as it is, or move it only together with a higher density floor at 200–650 m, and price that floor in the cost table.
- Make the plaza changes a post-layout overlay (separate fields written after `secondaryPaths` and the ponds), and prove the layout identity with a hash of `out.walks` and `out.ponds` against main.
- Choose the plaza type by plaza kind and hash, not by river distance, so river plazas also vary. Keep a lit element on every deck.
- Run the floating audit against the CDLOD height at the level used at each tree's distance, or bury each tree by a depth tied to that level's vertex spacing and the local slope.
- Leave street trees out of the species split, or limit them to the dome and upright oval.
