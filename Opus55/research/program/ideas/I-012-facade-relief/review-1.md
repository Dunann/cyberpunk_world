### Strongest reason it will fail
The A4 target is built mostly from parts the kits never touch, so A4 will most likely tie, and a tie fails adoption because both targets must win. I measured this instead of estimating it. In a scratch copy, I patched the facade shader to paint every face the kits could use (same eligibility rules as section B: RES/OFFICE/GLASS, kind 0–1, profile 0, taper 1, no twist or lean, at least 15 m, no NOWIN or LED, straight faces, balcony columns and floors only on `BALC` towers, within 149 m). I then rendered A4 and B2 through the real renderer at 1280x720, so every occluder (signs, podiums, props, other towers) is exact. Results:

| Pose | Eligible face pixels | Breakdown |
|---|---|---|
| A4 | about 4.5–5% of the frame (5.5% before removing sign-outline noise) | Balcony columns under 75 m: about 1.0%. Balcony columns at 75–149 m, already half sunk: 0.6%. One non-`BALC` residential wall (upper left, behind the shophouses, a long face): 2.8%. Other non-`BALC` faces at 75–149 m: 1.0%. Glass and office: 0. |
| B2 | about 17% | Glass under 75 m: 16.2% (the two walls on either side of the street). Glass at 75–149 m: 1.1%. |

- **What A4 actually shows.** All of these pixels are in the top quarter of the frame. The near buildings on both sides of the street are `MAT.SHOP` podiums: the right-hand shopfront with its upper glass floor and AC units, and the shophouses on the left. Together they cover roughly 20% of the frame and are excluded.
- **Why one roll decides A4.** The largest A4 candidate is the single non-`BALC` wall. It gets bay windows only if its tower wins the 60% roll. If it loses, A4 changes by under 2% of the frame, and the critics' A4 complaint ("flat shopfront glass panels") is not addressed.
- **The raw counts overstate the change.** The spec's +0.133M triangles and 346 chunks at A4 are the cull's view. Most of those elements are hidden behind podiums, signs and other towers.
- **B2 is a real change, but its sign is uncertain.** The left glass wall in B2 is the facade whose parallax office interiors already read as deep vertical relief at a grazing angle: the white "sawtooth" panels in the baseline image are fake-interior side walls, and they are the brightest detail in the shot. In this renderer, opaque "glass" fins 0.35 m deep every 2 m (every cell on podiums) cover about 0.35/tan(θ) of each 2 m bay. That is about 65% at 15° and nearly all of it beyond about 20 m along the wall. They would replace the lit interiors with a dark picket of fin sides. That could easily be judged a loss.

### Other concerns
- **Bay windows would black out lit windows.** The ARRAY fragment path is "cheap, with no `windowGrid`", and its material table has no bay-window glazing. Each bay box is (2·cw − 0.4) × 0.7·floorH and sits over two window cells, covering 75% of the stacks on every floor. At night the lit windows are what makes residential towers read. Shading the boxes as concrete or dark glass turns the lit facade into a grid of dark boxes. This hits the largest A4 candidate.
- **"Glass" fins and balustrades are opaque.** `mid 2` gives glass shading, not transparency. A dark, opaque, mirror-like "glass" balustrade or fin looks like a dark panel. Real glass balustrades and fins are mostly see-through, so these will not read as glass.
- **Laundry is removed for no reason.** Fading the painted laundry by `(1 − m)` removes a detail that sits on the wall above the balustrade, which is exactly where real laundry hangs inside a balcony. Keep it, and fade only the painted rail and the darkening.
- **ARRAY parts must skip the normal cull path.** The spec describes the ARRAY cull but never says ARRAY records must return before the list 0–5 logic. Without that, each ARRAY record draws as a ring-mesh box the size of the whole facade. Also, `Parts.add` sets `ROUND`/`CURVED` from `prm`, `prof` and `twist`. The spec stores depth in `c.z`, skip probability in `c.w` and `s` in `d.z`, so the post-pass must write raw data rather than call `add()`, or it will get those flags.
- **The cull has no whole-part reject.** As written, each ARRAY thread loops over every chunk (up to 1024) before any rejection, and the threads in a workgroup diverge. Test the whole-part bounding sphere and distance D first, and loop over chunks only for parts that pass.
- **Growing the record touches every part and every stride.** Going from 24 to 28 floats grows the buffer read by every building vertex, fragment and cull thread by 17%, for about 10k records out of 144k. It also touches every `* 24` stride while I-014 and I-015 prototypes are being built in the same files. A side buffer for array parameters, indexed from a field ARRAY parts do not use (for example `f.w`), avoids both.
- **The stop rule may be inside the noise.** The 0.08 ms bench threshold has no stated noise level. Bench runs are being repeated right now (`bench-base-6`, `bench-base-7` were running while I reviewed), which suggests run-to-run spread matters. One run against one run cannot resolve 0.08 ms if the spread is about 0.1 ms.
- **The held-out cameras are not fully fixed.** "Looking along the road" has two directions, and pitch is not given. An implementer who chooses after seeing the kits is tuning to the held-out cameras. I checked the seed points: (−450, −400) is in the Yuzhong Peninsula district (ground 57.5 m), and (3300, −2000) is in Lujiazui CBD. Both are fine choices.
- **Intersections are not handled.** Hillside cross-plan wings (12% of towers, `genHillside`) cross the main tower's faces. Skybridges (`skybridges`, up to 34 m gaps) attach to tower faces. The wing itself is a non-`BALC` residential part, so it is a bay-window candidate. Elements will cut through wings and bridge ends unless the post-pass skips any element whose box overlaps another solid part.
- **List numbering is unclear.** `LISTS = 6` today (lists 0–5), and the old list 6 belonged to I-005, which is dead. Either call it list 6, or state that the args buffer grows to 8 entries with a dead slot 6.
- **The sink makes facades "breathe" in motion.** Depth changes continuously from 75 to 149 m. During camera motion, balconies visibly grow out of the wall. This does not affect stills, but `burst-u12` and the fly-through will show it, together with thin 0.05 m balustrades and 0.12 m fins at 1–2 px. The motion vectors are static (`motionVector(wp, wp)`), so TAA will reproject the moving geometry slightly wrongly.
- **Things the spec gets right:**
  - The painted balcony rule reduces to column parity: `fract((c + 41·fid)·0.5 + 0.25·h) ≥ 0.5` exactly when `c + 41·fid` is odd. The JS twin is trivial and has no f32 precision risk.
  - Bits 22–23 fit under 2^24, and pid stays under 2^18 (about 144k parts).
  - The GPU cost on the gated bench average is credible. The fly-through runs at 25–330 m, and the kits stop at 149 m, so +0.02 to +0.1 ms is a fair range even with thin-triangle inefficiency. The p99 and worst-frame claims are reasonable.
- **No tuning to the eval cameras found.** The code uses no pose coordinates, kit choices come from part data and a separate seed, A4 and B2 are captured once at the end, and go/no-go uses held-out cameras. The 149 m distance comes from pixel size, not from the poses.

### What would make it stronger
- **Put the relief where street cameras look.** Add a street-face kit for the upper floors (above 4.6 m) of `MAT.SHOP` podiums in hillside and urban districts. It could use window surrounds and sills, protruding window cages in the cells where the painted cage rule already puts cages, and slabs or balconies on the podium windows. This is system-level, since every podium gets it. It roughly quadruples the A4 pixels that change, and it attacks the critics' actual A4 complaint.
- **Turn the painted cages into the "bay window" kit.** Chongqing and Hong Kong window cages are real protruding boxes. A cage box whose front shows the same cell's window (lit state from the same `winLit` id) with bars is more on-brief than a plain bay box, and it keeps the window light.
- **On B2, use metal fins and spare the lit podium interiors.** Use light-catching metal fins every second cell on glass towers only, and on podiums use canopies and perhaps a deeper transom ledge instead of fins at 2 m pitch.
- **Use a separate pipeline from the start.** It removes the uber-shader register-pressure risk to the 2.55 ms gbuffer pass. Keep the GEOBALC handover as the only change to the main `fs`.

### Required fixes (for a revision, or for a prototype if the coordinator sends it there)
1. **Kit coverage at A4.** Add the `MAT.SHOP` podium upper-floor street-face kit described above, chosen by part data only (never by position). Re-run the occlusion count and report the share of the A4, B2, H1 and H2 frames that changes. Paint the eligible faces in the shader and capture with the real renderer, as in this review. Target at least 15% of the A4 frame.
2. **Window light.** Every kit element that covers a window must show that cell's window light: the same `winLit` id and lit state, with emission and either the glass and frame or the cage bars. No opaque box may cover a lit window cell. Bay windows without this are dropped.
3. **Fins.** Drop opaque "glass" fins. Use metal fins every second cell on glass towers only. On glass podiums with lit office interiors, use canopies (and optionally a transom ledge) instead of fins.
4. **Laundry.** Keep the painted laundry on `GEOBALC` parts. Fade only the painted rail and the balcony darkening by `(1 − m)`.
5. **Cull path.** ARRAY parts return before the list 0–5 path. The ARRAY branch first rejects the whole part by frustum and by nearest-point distance against D, then loops over chunks. Add a dev assertion that pid < 2^18 and that chunks per part ≤ 1024.
6. **Record layout.** Do not grow `PART_FLOATS`. Store array parameters (counts, pitches, depth, skip probability, s) in a separate storage buffer indexed from an unused field of the ARRAY record. The post-pass writes records directly, not through `Parts.add`, so `ROUND`, `CURVED` and `NOBOX` are never set on ARRAY parts.
7. **Pipeline.** Draw list 7 (or list 6, renumbered) with its own pipeline and a small `vs`/`fs`. Include it in the depth prepass, so wall pixels hidden behind elements are not shaded twice. The only change to the main `fs` is the GEOBALC handover.
8. **Intersections.** The post-pass skips any element whose box overlaps another solid part (wings, skybridges, neighbouring towers), any element in a bay with an AC prop, and any face with a sign slot within 2 m.
9. **Held-out cameras.** Fix them completely before any code: yaw toward the road direction with the larger +x component (H2) and toward higher ground (H1), and pitch +8°. Write the exact x, y, z, yaw and pitch into the spec folder now, and never change them.
10. **Stop rule.** Measure bench noise first: three baseline runs and three candidate runs, interleaved, on a quiet GPU. Use the mean difference, and require it to be at most 0.08 ms and smaller than twice the baseline run-to-run standard deviation, or below 0.15 ms if that is larger. Also report gbuffer `--stats` at A4, B2, H1 and H2.
11. **Motion check.** Capture `burst-u12` and a 4-frame burst at H1 while the camera moves toward a balcony tower. Thin-element shimmer or visible "breathing" is a stop condition. If it appears, raise s for fins and balustrades so they sink earlier.
