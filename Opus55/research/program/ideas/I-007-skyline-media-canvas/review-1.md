### Strongest reason it will fail
The main effect, "one message reads across the gaps between towers", will not read on this skyline. The canvas only lands on pixels that are LED-flagged and whose face passes the face test. LED flags go to 35% of CBD towers (`buildings_gen.js:1001`, `R() < 0.35`) and to 30% of river-facing hillside and urban towers in step 3. The towers stand on a 125 m street grid, not in one riverfront row, so nearer towers block parts of farther ones. A 50–110 m glyph is about as wide as one 40–60 m CBD tower plus a gap. So each character gets cut into pieces, and most of the pieces land on towers that are not LED-flagged and stay dark. From B1 the judge would see blocky coloured fragments, not a message, which is no clearer than today's colour bands (see B1 in the baseline: the LED bands already read well). Real Lujiazui and Chongqing shows read as one message only because every tower in the front row takes part. On top of that, half the target poses are in standby. There the content is 0.35–0.5 brightness columns, and the skyline message is visible only 6 s in every 40 s. So either those poses see almost nothing new, or the cue phase ends up chosen so the message is lit at t = 100. That would be tuning to the eval cameras, because a pose's capture time is part of the pose.

### Other concerns
- **GPU cost: probably fits the gate, but the 0.1–0.2 ms estimate is not credible as written.** The estimate counts ALU per LED pixel (which is cheap: about 1M pixels × 60 operations is about 0.01 ms). It leaves out the real risk. `buildings_wgsl.js` is a single ~1000-line shader for every facade, drawn after a depth prepass (`buildings.js:165–175`). Adding a uniform block, a texture, a sampler, `atan2`, a cue list and a second LED path to both the glass and RES/OFFICE branches raises register use for all building pixels, not just LED ones. On a laptop RTX 4050 that can cost 0.1–0.3 ms of gbuffer time (2.55 ms average today). Lighting is already the largest pass (3.51 ms average, 5033 lights, `TILE_MAX = 256`). 80 lights with 30–40 m radius at 15–100 m height spread across many tiles in aerial views, where 2.5D culling helps least. My estimate is 0.15–0.5 ms in total, which fits the ~1.36 ms of headroom but leaves less room for I-005, I-006 and I-008, which also add to gbuffer. The quick test's "less than 0.1 ms added to gbuffer" check is taken at chosen views. It should be the full `bench.mjs` fly-through, since that is the gate.
- **Atlas memory:** raising H from 3072 to 4096 in rg8 adds 4096 × 1024 × 2 B = 8 MB at mip 0, plus about 2.7 MB of mips, so about 11 MB, not 4 MB. Harmless, but the spec should be corrected.
- **Canvas centres must come from the rule, not from named places.** The plan's rule (nearest river centerline point, pushed 150 m further over the water) is generic. But the Idea section names "Canvas 1: centred on the confluence" and "Canvas 2: the river point nearest the CBD", which invites hand placement. Using `RIVERS` and the CBD tower centroid (about 2800, −1100), the rule puts C near (2145, −1100), on the Bund edge about 230 m from the B1 camera (2080, −880). That is the one point from which a cylinder canvas reads correctly. It is defensible: real shows are designed for the opposite promenade. But the 150 m constant must not be tuned while looking at B1 or A3 captures, and code review should check that no centre or offset is a literal. The existing `ledColor` already hard-codes a centre at (3300, −1250). Do not copy that pattern.
- **Standby timing is a gaming risk.** A3, A6 and B3 are captured at t = 100, and the show runs over t ∈ [20, 80] of each 180 s cycle (`world.js:101–102`). The spec mentions only A3. The cue schedule phase and period must be fixed before any eval capture, and the quick test and checks should capture each target at several times (for example t = 100, 115, 130) to show the standby gain does not depend on a lucky phase.
- **The residential line-grid LED is invisible at eval distances.** At A6 (about 0.4–1.5 km away; vertical FOV is 1.05 rad, so about 0.6 m per pixel at 800 m), 0.25 m lines are far below one pixel. They fade to the cell average (about 15% coverage) × 0.35–0.5 standby brightness, which is a faint tint. Either A6 gains nothing from it, or it gets boosted into a flat colour wash. If it stays, it should be a separate, measured step, not part of the A6 claim.
- **Legibility floor.** Thresholding the R channel per cell at 50 m glyphs on 4 m floors gives about 12 rows. Bold YaHei strokes are about 11% of the glyph height, so about 1.3 rows, and strokes will break up. Set the glyph size in LED rows (at least 20 rows), not in metres. The phrase list includes complex characters (霓, 虹, 汇, 欢迎来到重庆) that the spec's own risk section says will not read.
- **Aliasing at distance.** The plan samples one atlas texel per cell with a hard threshold. When cells become smaller than a pixel (B3's far bank, G1, burst-u62), the on/off pattern aliases and will shimmer under TAA jitter. The glass branch needs the same fade the RES branch gets: sample a mip chosen from the cell's screen size and use the soft mask once cells are under about 2 px.
- **WGSL uniformity.** `fwidth` (RES branch) cannot be called inside the `flags & 4` branch, because the flags come from the per-part storage buffer and so are not uniform. Reuse the `fw` computed before the branch, as the current LED code does. `textureSampleLevel` is fine.
- **Rooftop letters with `discard`.** Adding `discard` to the shared `SIGN_WGSL` fragment shader turns off early-Z for every sign drawn with that pipeline. Kind 6 should get its own pipeline.
- **G1 is overstated.** G1 frames the peninsula 1–3 km away, with the CBD as a small strip about 4.4 km away at the top right. There, mega neon 4–7 m wide is 2–4 px wide and rooftop letters 6–14 m tall are 3–8 px. Expect a tie at G1.
- **Three ideas in one spec.** The canvas, the mega neon and the rooftop letters each change many poses. If the bundle loses one non-target pose (A1 or G3 looking cluttered), there is no way to tell which part caused it. That wastes an eval and the 2–3 implementer days. `towerFaces` also has to be published from five generators plus the landmarks without calling `R()`, which is more work than the plan suggests.
- **Merge overlap.** I-006 ("windows light in coherent patterns") changes the same facade emission and the same branches of `buildings_wgsl.js`. Its lit-window patterns and this canvas would compete on the same pixels.

### What would make it stronger
- Make **every river-facing tower in a canvas cluster LED-flagged** (by hash, without RNG), not 30–35%. Make **one character per tower face** (cue b) the main text cue, with the glyph fitted to that face's LED grid. Keep the cross-tower scroll as a secondary cue for faces that line up. This is what makes real shows readable, and it stays legible from off-axis views.
- Give the standby programme a **visible baseline at all times**: for example, a constant dim single-face column on 1–2 towers per cluster. Do not rely on a 6 s-in-40 s message. State the schedule before any capture, and show target poses at several capture times.
- Split the spec. **I-007a**, the canvas on CBD and landmark glass towers (targets B1, B3, B4, A3), is the cheap and testable core. **I-007b**, mega neon and rooftop letters (target A6 plus a check of non-target poses for clutter), would go only if 007a wins.
- Drop the residential line-grid LED from the first pass, or justify it with a capture at A6 distance.
- In the quick test, add: a held-out camera on the CBD bank looking back toward the Bund, to check the canvas is not one-sided; captures at t = 100 and t = 130; and the full `bench.mjs` run instead of a per-view `--stats` reading.
- Replace the GPU estimate with a measured number for the shader change alone (bind the atlas and uniform, add the code path, all LED flags off) to isolate the register-pressure cost before any content is added.
- Correct the atlas memory figure (about 11 MB), and write the canvas-centre rule so it contains no place names or coordinates.
