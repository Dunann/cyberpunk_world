# Engineering brief (shared by all module engineers)

You are one of five engineers building **Neon Confluence**, a real-time, explorable cyberpunk river city in the browser.

**Constraints**
- WebGPU, with no engine or library.
- Everything is procedural: no image, model, font or sound files. All geometry, textures and audio are generated by code.
- The deliverable is one self-contained `index.html`.

**Look and feel**
- **Inspiration:** Chongqing and Shanghai (skylines, river confluence, hillside density, Bund waterfront), with skyline features from Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur.
- **Culture:** a blend of China and Central Asia / Mongolia.
- **Nature district:** inspired by Suzhou gardens and Hangzhou's West Lake.
- **Setting:** mountainous terrain, at night, with light rain and neon.

**Priorities**
1. Visual quality and detail.
2. Stable FPS on the target machine: RTX 4050 Laptop (6 GB), 2560x1440 internal resolution, Chrome 157.
3. Immersion.

## Setup
- You work in your own git worktree, an isolated copy of the repo. The project root is `Opus55/source`.

  5. The placeholder versions of your own files.

## Engine facts
- **Coordinates:** meters. X = east, Y = up, Z = south (north is -Z).
  - The water surface is at Y = 0. Terrain below 0 is a river or lake bed.
  - The playable world is about ±5 km. The heightmap covers ±6144 m at 3 m per texel.
- **G-buffer:** this is a deferred renderer. Opaque shaders return:
  `gbuf(albedo, roughness, normal, metalness, emissiveHDR, ao, motionVector(worldPos, prevWorldPos), materialId, wetFactor)`
  - Material ids: 0 default, 1 foliage (wrap lighting), 2 glass (stronger reflection), 3 ground.
  - `wetFactor` (0..1) scales the global wetness. Wet surfaces get darker and glossier, and flat wet ground becomes mirror-like.
- **Motion vectors:** TAA needs them. Animated objects must pass their previous-frame world position: evaluate the animation at `time() - F.camFwd.w`. Static objects pass `motionVector(p, p)`.
- **Depth and winding:**
  - Depth uses reversed-Z with an infinite far plane: clear to 0, compare `greater`. `linearDepth(d)` gives view depth in meters.
  - Front faces are CCW when seen from outside (standard GL winding). `gbufferPipeline` culls back faces by default. Pass `cull: 'none'` for two-sided cards.
- **Local to world:** a lot's local frame maps to world with WGSL `rot2(angle) * local`, matching `World._buildLots` `toWorld`. Note that the placeholder `buildings.js` uses the wrong sign; ignore it.
- **Forward pass (transparents):**
  - There is one HDR color target (`rgba16float`) and a motion target. The motion write mask is 0 unless the pipeline sets `writeMotion: true`.
  - Depth is read-only and is bound as `depthTex`. `sceneTex` is a copy of the lit opaque scene. `gNormalTex` holds the G-buffer normals.
  - The `lights` array is also bound.
  - Call `applyFog()` in forward shaders.
- **Point lights:**
  - Use `ctx.lights.add(...)` at init for static lights, or `reserve(n)` plus `set(i, ...)` per frame for dynamic ones.
  - Tiled lighting caps each 16x16 pixel tile at 256 lights. Keep radii modest (about 10–40 m) and do not cluster hundreds of lights in one spot.
  - Every light costs shading time, so prefer emissive geometry plus a few lights.
  - Your share of the 8192-light total is given in your task. Stay within it.
- **Exposure and HDR levels:**
  - Typical values: lit windows about 0.5–3, neon tubes 4–15, the moonlit sky about 0.01–0.1.
  - The composite uses a neutral tonemapper and bloom, with `ctx.env.exposure = 1.6`.
- **Code rules:**
  - Write WGSL inside JS template literals prefixed with `/* wgsl */`; the build minifies them.
  - No per-frame JS allocations in `update`, `compute`, `gbuffer` or `forward` hooks: no new arrays, objects, closures or template strings per frame. Preallocate typed arrays and descriptors.
  - No external assets, no network, no libraries.
- **Quality and determinism:**
  - `ctx.quality.level` is 0 (low), 1 (medium) or 2 (high); scale density by it where useful.
  - Use `rng(seed)` from `math.js` so the scene is identical on every load.
- **Performance:**
  - Use instancing and GPU work.
  - Cull what is off screen or far away (CPU chunk culling against the camera is fine).
  - Use cheap distant LODs.
  - Avoid overdraw in the forward pass.

## Build and test
- `node build.mjs --dev` builds `out/dev.html`, a readable build with dev tools. `node build.mjs` builds the production `../index.html`. Both must succeed.
- `node tools/capture.mjs --poses 2,8 --jpg --profile opus55-<you> --out out/shots-<you>` captures viewpoints at 2560x1440 from a headed Chrome on the NVIDIA GPU.
  - Poses are the `POSES` array in `world.js`, numbered from 1.
  - `--stats` prints GPU ms per pass.
  - `--cam x,y,z,yaw,pitch[,t] --name foo` captures any custom camera.
    - yaw 0 looks north (-Z); positive yaw turns left (west).
    - Positive pitch looks up.
    - t is the scene time in seconds. The light show runs while 20 < (t mod 180) < 80.
  - `--query disable=buildings,signs` disables modules, so you can measure your own cost or isolate your work.
  - The tool prints any WebGPU validation errors. There must be none.
- Look at your captures with the Read tool. Critique them like an art director and iterate until they look great, both from far away and up close.
- **Ground truth for your own captures:**
  - Captures are frozen in time, with TAA converged after 48 frames.
  - Use a unique `--profile` name. Other engineers are capturing on the same GPU at the same time, so GPU timings can be noisy. Re-measure if a number looks wrong.
- Other engineers are building the other modules in parallel. In your worktree their modules are placeholders. Integrate only through the documented contracts.
